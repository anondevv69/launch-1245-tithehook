# Independent adversarial review — 2026-10-16

## What this review is, and is not

This is an **independent adversarial review by the agent team**: it was directed by fren (the
submission team's agent) and executed by reviewers who did **not** implement these contracts,
working from attack briefs. The implementation self-review in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) was
treated as a set of unproven claims to test, not as input trust.

It is **not** a paid third-party audit by an external security firm, and it is not an audit
certificate. It does not include formal verification (see "Not done", below).

**Headline result: no Critical and no confirmed High findings.** The arithmetic core (inverse
fee identity, overflow bounds, conservation) was verified with proofs and executable tests.
The material findings are economic properties of the disclosed design — chiefly the
exact-input tithe on partial fills (M-1) — plus documentation absolutes that do not hold in
two narrow windows (L-3). All findings about the deployed contracts are **disclosed, not
patched**: the contracts are immutable and have no admin, pause, upgrade or rescue path.

## 5. Findings

### High (conditional — retired for the launch pool)

**H-1. First-initializer-wins binding applies to every pool key, including the demo key.**
`initializePool` is permissionless and the first caller permanently binds a pool key to a
seat and rate of their choosing (`src/TitheHook.sol` `_configure`, `initializePool`); there
is no rebind or admin path. Had anyone initialized the demo ETH/TDEMO key before the launch
factory's atomic deploy-and-initialize, the demo pool could have been bound to an attacker's
seat at 9,999 bps. **This did not happen on the live deployment**: the demo pool's on-chain
config is seat 1 at 25 bps (§3) and the fork rehearsal executed the intended demo path. The
property remains live for every *future* pool key by design: whoever initializes first wins,
so any integrator must read `poolConfig(poolId)` before adding liquidity or routing — the
documentation already says this; treat it as load-bearing, not boilerplate.

### Medium

**M-1. Exact-input tithe is charged on the full specified budget, with a discontinuity at
zero consumption.** `beforeSwap` fixes `F = floor(A · r / BPS)` on the budget `A`
(`src/TitheHook.sol:136`, `:216-217`); `afterSwap` collects the same `F` for any nonzero
AMM consumption `x`, reverting only when `x == 0` (`:159-162`). The swapper's total never
exceeds their budget, and the unspent remainder is never debited — but the effective tithe
rate on the amount actually paid, `F/(x+F)`, approaches 100% as `x → 0⁺`. Proven by review
test 4: on a 9,999 bps pool, a 10 ETH budget with a one-tick price limit consumed
< 0.001 ETH in the AMM and still paid 9.999 ETH of tithe. At the demo's 25 bps the same
shape charges 1.25 ETH on the repo's own 500 ETH-budget / ~3.08 ETH-fill scenario
(`test/TitheGenericRouter.t.sol`). A counterparty who seeds a shallow pool bound to their
own seat profits from other swappers' oversized budgets; the swapper's only real defenses
are sizing budgets to the pool and a meaningful `minOutput` (the reference router enforces
only the caller-chosen minimum). This behavior is disclosed in `docs/ACCOUNTING.md` and
`docs/SECURITY.md`; the review confirms the disclosure is accurate and rates the property
Medium because it bites through canonical routers with ordinary settings.

**M-2. Claims pay the seat's owner at claim time; there is no accrual snapshot.** Credits
are keyed by `seatId` only (`src/TitheHook.sol:36-37`), and `claim` resolves
`IMD_SEAT.ownerOf(seatId)` after redemption (`:193`). Consequences, all verified in code
and tests: a seat sale races any pending claim (whoever's transaction lands second sets
who keeps the accrued tithe, independent of the agreed price); anyone who temporarily
holds the seat — an approved operator, an escrow, a rental — can claim the entire
historical accrual and return the seat in the same flow (the implementation's own
`SeatMovingToken` test demonstrates the operator leg). A seat moved *during* redemption
pays the new owner; moved *during* the payout transfer, the already-resolved owner is
paid; double payment is impossible (credit is zeroed before transfer, `:195-196`).
Disclosed in `docs/SECURITY.md`. Practical rule: bundle claim + transfer atomically in
any seat sale that prices in accrued tithe.

**M-3. The permitted rate ceiling (9,999 bps) makes exact-output fees 9,999× the AMM
input, and cap-less routers pay in full.** Exact-output fee is `input · r / (BPS − r)`
(`:164`): at 9,999 bps the gross is ~10,000× the AMM input. Review test 2 proves a
canonical SETTLE_ALL/TAKE_ALL router with no input cap pays it exactly and settles
cleanly. `TitheRouter` users are protected by its `maxInput` check (the swap reverts,
funds intact). Integrators must quote the gross (input + tithe) and read `poolConfig`
before routing to any pool (see H-1).

### Low

**L-1. Sub-threshold swaps are tithe-free, and splitting evasion is denominated in base
units.** Floor rounding makes the fee 0 whenever `A · r < 10000` (exact input) — at 25 bps,
budgets up to 399 base units pay nothing (the implementation's
`test_tinyInputRoundsFeeToZero` asserts this as intended). For the 18-decimal TDEMO this is
economically negligible. For a low/zero-decimal, high-unit-value currency admitted as a
pool currency, splitting a trade into sub-threshold swaps is a practical total evasion.
Rounding otherwise always favors the swapper by < 1 base unit per swap (V-1).

**L-2. `_collect`'s take-vs-mint branch is selected by the manager's aggregate cash at one
instant, and the unlock's composer controls it.** `deferred = currency.balanceOf(poolManager)
< fee` (`src/TitheHook.sol:243`). The review verified both branches are sound and equivalent
for standard tokens — no ordering creates an unbacked claim or spends another participant's
cash (the hook's take is offset by a debt the swapper must settle before the unlock can
close). For transfer-tax tokens the branches differ: the take path credits the measured
net amount now; the mint path credits face value and defers the tax to redemption, so
`titheOf` is nominal until redeemed (disclosed in `docs/ACCOUNTING.md`).

**L-3. Value arriving during the take measurement window is credited to the collecting
seat — the "donations are unassigned surplus" absolute does not hold in-window.** Proven
by review test 5: a token that mints a bonus to the hook during the manager→hook `take`
has that bonus measured as collection proceeds and credited to the seat whose swap is
collecting (likewise for the redemption window in `_redeemUnlocked`). Solvency is **not**
broken — the extra credit is backed 1:1 by the extra tokens — but surplus arriving inside
the window is assigned, not unassigned, contrary to the blanket wording in
`docs/ACCOUNTING.md`/`docs/SECURITY.md`. Requires a nonstandard (bonus/rebasing-in-transfer)
token; TDEMO is a plain ERC-20 and unaffected.

**L-4. Stranded-value classes exist by design, with no rescue.** A seat that is burned,
never minted, owned by the hook itself, or unable to receive a payout makes that seat's
credit unclaimable forever (claims revert, credit preserved — tested by the implementation
suite). Forced ETH and donated assets sent to the hook or to `TitheRouter` are likewise
unrecoverable surplus; a seat owned by `TitheRouter` or the PoolManager strands its
payouts the same way. There is deliberately no authority that could redirect any of it.

**L-5. Claim/redeem inside a third party's unlock works — but a reverting claim aborts the
host's whole unlock.** The `isUnlocked()` branch (`src/TitheHook.sol:262`) is exercised by
review test 1: exact payout, idempotent redemption, forgery attempts rejected. The
residual risk is compositional: any bundled claim that reverts (ETH-rejecting owner,
`RedeemPendingFirst` above one chunk) rolls back the host protocol's entire unlock,
including its swaps. Integrators should claim outside swap unlocks.

### Informational

**I-1. Above `int128.max` of pending tithe, `claim()` alone can never complete.** Its
internal redemption chunk is rolled back by its own `RedeemPendingFirst` revert
(`src/TitheHook.sol:185-189`, `:267-270`); progress requires separate `redeemTithe`
transactions first. No funds at risk; documented. Note the implementation's chunking test
runs against an accounting-only manager mock (`test/mocks/ClaimManager.sol`), since
accruing that much through a real pool is impractical — chunking against the real
PoolManager's delta accounting is untested (the fork-scale amounts are far below the cap).

**I-2. Integrator naming hazard:** `totalTithe(token)` counts liquid **plus** pending
liabilities, while `tithe(seatId, token)` counts liquid only. Internally consistent
everywhere traced; an integrator reading `totalTithe` as liquid backing would
double-count against `totalPending`.

**I-3. Verified sound — reentrancy architecture and payout integrity.** The shared
OpenZeppelin guard covers initialization, both swap callbacks, redemption and claims;
`unlockCallback` is manager-only plus the `_redeeming` flag, whose lifecycle cannot be
desynchronized (any failure rolls back the flag write with the transaction; the manager
only ever calls back the unlock's initiator, so the hook's callback cannot be forged or
fed attacker data). `claim` never takes a recipient from the caller and pays only the
live seat owner. Slither's reentrancy flags and Mythril's state-after-call flag are false
positives under this architecture (guard + immutable manager as the only external call
target in the flagged windows + effects zeroed before the payout transfer).

**I-4. Verified claims (proofs in the review workpapers).** (V-1) The exact-output identity
`floor(x·r/(B−r)) = floor((x+F)·r/B)` holds exactly, for all `x ≥ 0`, `1 ≤ r ≤ 9999`.
(V-2) Overflow headroom: the largest intermediate product is `< 2^141` against a `2^256`
word, and every narrowing cast is preceded by the bound check at `:168`. (V-3)
`beforeSwap`/`afterSwap` cannot disagree on the exact-input fee: it is a pure function of
the (manager-passed, immutable) params and the write-once config; swap `hookData` is
ignored by both callbacks. (V-4) Conservation holds across collect/redeem/claim
interleavings, including tax-shrunk redemptions and donated-claim surplus isolation.

**I-5. Process note.** During this review one reviewer initially transcribed the hook
address with four hex digits dropped, which blocked its live reads; the address of record
is the full 40-hex `0x815dE6EcfC1e0B24b7Ae8A5Dcc05ba8f5d8F20Cc` (checksummed), and all
on-chain results in §3–§4 were obtained with it.

## 6. Automated-tool triage

**Slither 0.11.6** (73 results over 39 contracts). In `src/`: 2× reentrancy-no-eth
(`claim`, `_redeemUnlocked`), 4× reentrancy-benign (`_collect`, `_redeemUnlocked`),
1× reentrancy-events — all **false positives** per I-3 (external calls go only to the
immutable PoolManager; every entry point is behind the shared guard; liabilities are
decremented before interactions on the payout path). 1× timestamp in `TitheRouter.swap` —
the deadline comparison is the intended semantics, informational. The remaining
Medium/Low results (unused-return ×6, divide-before-multiply, uninitialized-local,
incorrect-modifier) are all in the pinned upstream `lib/v4-core` code, not in `src/`;
the rest is informational lint (assembly, dead code in libraries, naming).

**Mythril 0.24.8** (deployed runtime bytecode): 14× "arithmetic operator can overflow"
(SWC-101 class) — **false positives**: the hook is compiled from Solidity 0.8.26 with
checked arithmetic, and V-2 proves the operand bounds Mythril's solver could not
discharge at depth 64. 1× Low "write to persistent state following external call" —
the same pattern class as the Slither reentrancy flags; false positive per I-3.
